0% VAT on electricity: what it means for your bill

Household electricity in England, Scotland and Wales has a temporary zero rate of VAT until 31 March 2027. Here’s how it works.

By the EnergySavers editorial team · 2 October 2026 · Updated 15 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Dan LeFebvre on Unsplash

The key facts

  • Announced on 21 July 2026; applies from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027.
  • Covers electricity for domestic use in Great Britain. It does not apply in Northern Ireland.
  • Gas and other domestic fuels stay at 5% VAT.
  • It’s applied automatically by your supplier, including on fixed tariffs.
  • The government estimates a typical household saves around £45 a year.

What happens in April 2027?

Unless the government extends it, VAT on household electricity goes back to 5% on 1 April 2027. That would push electricity prices up by about 5% on that date, on top of any change in the price cap. Keep an eye on our what’s changing calendar.
