Energy price cap January 2027: when it’s announced and what to do now

Ofgem sets a new price cap every three months, and the next one covers 1 January to 31 March 2027. Nobody outside Ofgem knows the figure yet, but you can use the days before it is published to get your meter readings and tariff in order.

By the EnergySavers editorial team · 18 November 2026 · 3 min read

When is the January 2027 price cap announced?

Ofgem’s published timetable lists 25 November 2026 as the announcement date for the cap that applies from 1 January to 31 March 2027. Ofgem says it can publish earlier if external reasons require it, so check its news page rather than relying on social media posts claiming to have the new figures.

As of November 2026 the cap in force is the 1 October to 31 December 2026 level: a typical household paying by direct debit is capped at £1,723 a year, with electricity at 26.32p per kWh plus a 54.83p daily standing charge, and gas at 7.97p per kWh plus a 29.68p daily standing charge (Ofgem). The full numbers and history are on our price cap page.

What the price cap does and doesn’t limit

The cap is a limit on the unit rates and standing charges a supplier can charge on a standard variable (default) tariff. Ofgem is clear that it does not limit your total bill: use more energy and you pay more, whatever the cap is.

  • Covered: households on standard variable tariffs.
  • Not covered: fixed tariffs, business contracts, and homes that use a heat network or heating oil.

The “£1,723” style headline is an annual figure for a typical household, using Ofgem’s typical domestic consumption values. Your own bill depends on your usage, region and payment method.

What goes into the cap

Ofgem builds the cap from several cost blocks: wholesale energy bought on the open market, network costs for the pipes and wires, policy costs such as funding for government schemes including the Warm Home Discount, operating costs, supplier earnings and an allowance for uncertain costs. That is why the cap can move even when wholesale prices are steady.

Tax matters too. Electricity is at 0% VAT from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027 in Great Britain (not Northern Ireland), while gas stays at 5%. Our VAT cut explainer covers what that means for your bill, and the relief is currently scheduled to end on 31 March 2027.

Will bills go up or down in January?

We don’t know, and we won’t guess a figure. Forecasters publish estimates before each announcement: in September, Cornwall Insight forecast around £1,872 for January to March 2027, but that was a forecast made weeks ago and it can change as wholesale prices move. Treat any figure that isn’t from Ofgem as an estimate until 25 November.

The sensible approach is to prepare for either direction rather than bet on one.

What to do before the announcement

  1. Send meter readings. A reading at the end of the current cap period means you are billed for the right usage at the right rates. See how to read your gas and electricity meter.
  2. Check what you are on. If you are on a standard variable tariff your rates will follow the new cap. If you are on a fixed deal, find the end date and the exit fee.
  3. Compare calmly. If you are weighing a fix, read should you fix your energy this winter and use our guide to switching supplier. Check any offer against the cap’s unit rates and the standing charge, not just the headline.
  4. Check for help. Winter support such as the Warm Home Discount doesn’t depend on the cap figure. See our grants page.

Frequently asked questions

When is the next Ofgem price cap announced?
Ofgem’s timetable lists 25 November 2026 for the cap that runs from 1 January to 31 March 2027. Check Ofgem’s website on the day for the official figures.
Does the price cap limit my total energy bill?
No. It limits the unit rates and standing charges on standard variable tariffs. Your total bill still depends on how much energy you use.
Does the price cap apply if I am on a fixed tariff?
No. Fixed tariffs are not covered by the cap, so your rates stay as agreed until the deal ends.
Is the January 2027 price cap figure known yet?
Not as of 18 November 2026. Any figure you see before Ofgem’s announcement is a forecast or an estimate.
