Landlords and EPC C by 2030: what’s been decided

The government has confirmed new minimum energy efficiency standards for private rented homes. Here’s the timetable and what it means for landlords and tenants.

By the EnergySavers editorial team · 10 October 2026 · Updated 15 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: William V on Unsplash

The rules

  • Private rented homes in England and Wales must reach EPC band C by 1 October 2030, unless a valid exemption applies.
  • A single deadline for all tenancies: the earlier proposal for 2028 for new tenancies was dropped.
  • A cost cap of £10,000 per property, with exemptions valid for 10 years.
  • Homes already rated C under the current rating can count as compliant until their certificate expires, for certificates issued before 1 October 2029.
  • Short-term lets are outside the rules for now.

What landlords should do now

Check your current EPC, get advice on the cheapest route to band C (often insulation, heating controls or a heat pump), and look at the Boiler Upgrade Scheme and Warm Homes Plan support. The government says low-interest loans will be available.

What renters can expect

Better-insulated homes should be cheaper to heat. Until then, tenants who pay their own energy bills can usually switch supplier and use our energy saving tips.
