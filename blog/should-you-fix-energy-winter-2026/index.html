Should you fix your energy this winter?

Forecasts suggest the price cap will rise again in January. Here’s how to decide whether a fixed tariff makes sense for you.

By the EnergySavers editorial team · 12 October 2026 · Updated 15 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Jon Moore on Unsplash

The case for fixing

  • Forecasters expect the January 2027 cap to be higher than October’s £1,723, with Cornwall Insight’s September forecast around £1,872.
  • Ofgem said in August that fixes were available at £100 or more below the October cap.
  • A fix gives certainty through the coldest months.

The case against

  • If wholesale prices fall, you could be stuck paying more, or pay an exit fee to leave.
  • Forecasts change: the January cap isn’t set until late November.
  • A fix priced above the current cap only pays off if prices rise enough.

A simple rule of thumb

A fix at or below today’s cap, with low or no exit fees, has little downside. A fix well above the cap is a bet on rising prices. Run the numbers with your own kWh using our deal checklist.
