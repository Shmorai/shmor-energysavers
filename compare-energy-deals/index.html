How to compare energy deals

A deal is only a good deal if it beats what you’d pay anyway. Here’s how to check any fixed or variable tariff against the price cap and your real usage.

Photo: Sarah Agnew on Unsplash

The five-minute deal check

  1. Get your annual kWh

    From your bill or app. Use kWh, not pounds: it’s the only fair way to compare.

  2. Note both rates

    Unit rate and standing charge, for gas and electricity separately.

  3. Benchmark the cap

    Compare with the current cap for your region and payment method.

  4. Check the term

    How long is it fixed, and what’s the exit fee per fuel?

  5. Read the extras

    Smart meter requirement, online-only service, bundles or perks.

Work out what a deal really costs you

Annual cost = (your kWh × unit rate) + (365 × standing charge), for each fuel. Do the same with price cap rates to see the saving. A deal that looks cheap per kWh can lose its advantage if the standing charge is high and your usage is low.

Example method: if you use 2,700 kWh of electricity a year, multiply by the unit rate in pounds, then add 365 days of standing charge. Repeat for gas. Compare the total with the same calculation at price cap rates.

Should you fix this winter?

Fixing protects you from rises but means you won’t benefit if prices fall. With forecasters expecting the January 2027 cap to be higher than October’s, many households are looking at fixes. Ofgem said in August that fixes were available at £100 or more below the October cap. Things to weigh up:

  • Is the fix at or below the current cap? If it’s above, you’re paying for insurance against rises.
  • How long is the term, and what are the exit fees?
  • Remember the 0% VAT on electricity is due to end on 31 March 2027; fixed prices usually move with VAT changes.

Where to find live deals

Prices change daily, so we don’t publish tariff prices that could be out of date. Use an Ofgem-accredited price comparison site (accredited under the Confidence Code), or check suppliers’ own websites. Some suppliers’ cheapest deals are only available direct or to existing customers.

Frequently asked questions

Why don’t you list live tariff prices?
Energy prices change daily and vary by region, meter and payment method. Rather than show prices that may be wrong, we explain how to compare and point you to accredited comparison sites and suppliers.
Is switching energy supplier free?
Yes. Switching itself is free. You may pay an exit fee if you leave a fixed deal early, except in the last 49 days of the fix.
Should I switch if I have a smart meter?
Yes, you can. Most smart meters keep working in smart mode after a switch, and newer meters connect to the national smart network.
