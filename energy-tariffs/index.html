Which tariff type should you choose?

  • You want certainty: a fixed tariff, ideally priced at or below the current price cap.
  • You think prices will fall: stay on standard variable or a tracker, and accept that rates can also rise.
  • You have an EV, battery or heat pump: a time-of-use, EV or heat pump tariff usually beats everything else if you can shift usage.
  • You use very little energy: look at the Ofgem lower standing charge pilot tariffs.
  • You have storage heaters: check you are on Economy 7 or 10, and that your heaters charge during the cheap hours.

Sources: Ofgem: lower standing charge tariffs, next steps · Ofgem: Smart Export Guarantee.

Unit rates vs standing charges

Every tariff has two parts. The unit rate is what you pay per kWh. The standing charge is a fixed daily amount that covers network and supplier costs, whether you use energy or not. A tariff with a low unit rate but a high standing charge can cost more if you use little energy, so always compare both for your own annual usage.

Frequently asked questions

Are fixed tariffs cheaper than the price cap?
Sometimes. In August 2026 Ofgem said fixes were available at £100 or more below the October cap. Prices change daily, so compare any fix with the current cap and with forecasts for the next one.
Can I leave a fixed tariff early?
Yes, but you may pay an exit fee. Suppliers can’t charge exit fees if you switch in the last 49 days of a fixed deal.
Do I need a smart meter for a time-of-use tariff?
Yes. Tracker, agile, EV and most time-of-use tariffs need a smart meter that sends half-hourly readings.
