The UK energy price cap right now

From 1 October to 31 December 2026 the Ofgem price cap is equivalent to £1,723 a year for a typical dual fuel household paying by Direct Debit, up 4% on the summer. Electricity is capped at an average of 26.32p per kWh with a 54.83p daily standing charge; gas at 7.97p per kWh and 29.68p a day. VAT on household electricity in Great Britain is temporarily 0% until 31 March 2027.

The cap limits the rates, not your total bill. Read our price cap guide for what it means for you and what forecasters expect in January, or see everything that’s changing.

Compare suppliers, tariffs and deals properly

Around a third of households are on fixed deals, and Ofgem says fixes have been available at £100 or more below the October cap. But the cheapest headline deal isn’t always the best value for you. Our guides show you how to compare:

  • Suppliers: who supplies what, from the largest firms to green and app-based challengers, in our UK energy suppliers guide.
  • Tariffs: fixed, variable, tracker, time-of-use, Economy 7, EV and prepay, in our tariff cards.
  • Deals: how to judge a deal against the cap, exit fees and standing charges, in how to compare energy deals.
  • Switching: your rights and the timeline, in how to switch energy supplier.

Water bills: you can’t switch, but you can still save

Households in England and Wales can’t choose their water company, and average bills rose again in April 2026. You can still save with a water meter, a social tariff or a capped bill under WaterSure. The water industry is also being reformed, with Ofwat due to be replaced by a single new regulator.

Frequently asked questions

Is EnergySavers an energy supplier or switching site?
No. We are an independent guide and comparison site. You switch or sign up directly with the supplier, or through a switching service, on their terms.
Where do your price cap figures come from?
From Ofgem, the energy regulator for Great Britain. They are national averages for Direct Debit customers; your own rates depend on your region, meter and payment method.
Do you cover Northern Ireland and Scotland?
The Ofgem price cap covers England, Scotland and Wales. Northern Ireland has a separate energy market and the temporary 0% VAT on electricity does not apply there. Water is supplied by Scottish Water and NI Water, and we explain how bills work in each nation.
Is this financial advice?
No. Our guides are general information. Check the provider, Ofgem or GOV.UK before you decide, and talk to Citizens Advice if you’re struggling to pay.
