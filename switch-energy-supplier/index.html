Switching guide

How to switch energy supplier

Switching is free, your supply never goes off, and your new supplier does almost all the work. Here’s what happens and what your rights are.

Photo: Andrey Metelev on Unsplash

What happens when you switch

  1. Choose a tariff and sign up with the new supplier (or through a comparison site).
  2. The new supplier contacts your old one. You don’t need to.
  3. You have a 14-day cooling-off period to change your mind.
  4. Give meter readings on the switch day (smart meters send them automatically).
  5. Your old supplier sends a final bill. Any credit should be refunded.

Most switches complete within a few working days of the cooling-off period, and your gas and electricity stay on throughout: the pipes and wires don’t change.

Your rights when switching

  • No exit fees in the last 49 days of a fixed tariff.
  • Prepayment with debt: you can usually switch with up to £500 of debt per fuel (£1,000 dual fuel), which moves to the new supplier.
  • Credit refunds: your old supplier should refund credit after the final bill.
  • Renters: if you pay the energy bills, you can usually switch, but check your tenancy agreement.

Sources: Citizens Advice: energy supply.

When switching might not be right

If your fixed deal is cheaper than anything available now, staying put may be best. If you owe your supplier money on a credit meter, you may need to clear the debt before you can leave. If you’re struggling, talk to your supplier about a payment plan and see our grants and support guide.

Last updated: 15 October 2026

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to switch energy supplier?
Usually a few working days after the 14-day cooling-off period, unless you ask to switch sooner. Your supply is never interrupted.
Do I need to tell my old supplier?
No. Your new supplier arranges the switch. Just give accurate meter readings on the switch date.
Can I switch if I’m in debt to my supplier?
Prepayment customers can usually switch with up to £500 of debt per fuel. Credit meter customers often need to repay first. Ask your supplier for help if you’re struggling.
