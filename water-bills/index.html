Water & sewerage bills

Water bills: find your company and save

You can’t switch water supplier at home in England and Wales, but you can still cut your bill. Here’s who supplies your water, how meters work and where to get help.

Who supplies your water?

Water companies are regional monopolies. Some supply both water and sewerage; in some areas a separate “water only” company supplies your water while a larger company handles sewerage, so you may get two bills.

Water bills in 2026

Bills in England and Wales rose again from April 2026, as companies pay for investment in pipes, reservoirs and sewage treatment. Water UK forecast an average household rise of about £33 (5.4%), to around £639 a year, with big differences between companies.

Sources: MoneySavingExpert: water bills to rise from April.

Would a water meter save you money?

Without a meter you pay a fixed charge based on your home’s old “rateable value”. With a meter you pay for what you use. In England and Wales you can usually ask your water company to fit a meter for free. As a rule of thumb, a meter often saves money if there are fewer people in your home than bedrooms.

  • If the meter doesn’t save you money, you can usually switch back to unmetered charges within a set period (typically 12 months).
  • If a meter can’t be fitted, ask for an “assessed charge”, which can be lower.
  • Use CCW’s water meter calculator before you decide.

Help if you can’t afford your water bill

  • Social tariffs: every company in England and Wales has a scheme cutting bills for lower-income households. Rules and discounts vary by company.
  • WaterSure (WaterSure Plus / WaterSure Wales): caps a metered bill if you get certain benefits and have a large family or a medical condition that means using more water.
  • Payment breaks and plans: ask your water company early. Many have hardship funds and can clear arrears if you keep up regular payments.
  • Free advice: the Consumer Council for Water (CCW) helps with disputes and support.

The water industry is being reformed

In July 2025 the government announced that Ofwat will be abolished and replaced by a single new water regulator, combining water functions now spread across Ofwat, the Environment Agency, Natural England and the Drinking Water Inspectorate. A white paper, A new vision for water, followed in January 2026, with legislation to follow. Campaigners have criticised the decision not to include a single national social tariff. Frequently asked questions

Can I switch water supplier?
Not as a household in England, Wales or Scotland: your water company depends on where you live. Businesses in England and Scotland can choose their water retailer.
Is a water meter cheaper?
Often, especially if fewer people live in your home than there are bedrooms. In England and Wales you can usually switch back within a set period if it doesn’t save money.
What is a water social tariff?
A discounted bill for households on low incomes. Each water company runs its own scheme, so eligibility and discounts vary. Ask your company or CCW.
